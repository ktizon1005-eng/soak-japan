# Can You Go to an Onsen in Japan With Tattoos? Here's What the Official Rules Say (October 2026)

*Short version of a full guide on Soak Japan.*

If you have a tattoo and you're planning a trip to Japan, you've probably read that onsen will turn you away. That used to be close to true. In 2026 it depends on the place — and many blogs are out of date.

We checked the official website of each place below on October 9, 2026. Here's what we found.

**1. Some places welcome tattoos as they are.** All seven public baths in Kinosaki Onsen allow tattoos of all sizes, according to the town's official tourism site.

**2. Many big Tokyo-area bathhouses now use a "sticker rule".** You buy the facility's own cover stickers and cover the tattoo completely. Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club, for example, lets you in if the tattoo fits under up to two of its seals (each up to 11 × 20 cm). Shinjuku's Thermae-yu admits men only if they're foreign tourists wearing its sticker and showing a passport.

**3. Some still say no — stickers included.** Spa World in Osaka refuses anyone with a tattoo, stickers or not.

**4. Some popular recommendations are out of date.** Jakotsu-yu in Asakusa, still recommended on many sites, has closed for good. Hakone Yunessun tightened its rules in October 2025.

**5. A private bath avoids the question entirely.** Japan's tourism agency lists private baths as one way to welcome guests with tattoos.

Rules change, so always check the facility's own website before you go.
