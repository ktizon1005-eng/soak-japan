**This guide is for you if** it's your first time at a Japanese onsen and you're worried about being naked in front of strangers or doing something wrong. By the end you'll know exactly what happens from the moment you walk in to the moment you leave, so you can relax instead of guessing.

Everything here comes from official sources: Japan's national tourism organisation (JNTO), the Japan Onsen Association, and city tourism offices. Links are included so you can check them yourself.

## Yes, you bathe naked

This is the part most people worry about, so let's start here. JNTO says onsen bathing is typically enjoyed in the nude, and clothing, including swimwear, stays in the changing area ([JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/bathing-manners-and-tips/)). The Japan Onsen Association's etiquette poster says the same: take off underwear and swimwear before you bathe ([Japan Onsen Association](https://www.spa.or.jp/news/book/3248/)).

If you really can't do it, see the end of this guide for other options.

## What happens, step by step

1. **Changing room.** Undress completely and leave your clothes in a locker or basket. Leave your phone here too (more on that below).
2. **Take only the small towel.** A small towel is usually provided ([JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/bathing-manners-and-tips/)). In some places, such as Beppu's public baths, nothing is provided and you should bring your own towel and soap ([Beppu City](https://beppu-tourism.com/en/feature02/)).
3. **Wash before you get in.** Sit at a shower station, wash thoroughly and rinse off all the soap before entering the water ([JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/responsible-travel-guide/japanese-customs-and-etiquette/)). The Onsen Association also suggests pouring hot water over yourself 3–5 times first.
4. **Soak.** Keep the towel out of the water: put it on your head or on the side of the bath ([JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/bathing-manners-and-tips/)).

## The rules that matter most

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Wash and rinse before getting in | Put your towel in the water |
| Tie up long hair or wrap it in a towel | Swim, splash or jump in |
| Speak softly | Take photos — ever |
| Leave your phone outside the bathing area | Bathe after drinking alcohol |

Sources: [JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/bathing-manners-and-tips/), [Japan Onsen Association](https://www.spa.or.jp/news/book/3248/), [Sapporo Tourism Association](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/info/useful_information/how-to-bathe-at-an-onsen/).

Photos deserve a special mention: JNTO calls taking photos in the bath "absolutely forbidden", and the Sapporo tourism office says never to take photos in the changing room either. Leaving your phone in the locker solves this.

## Stay safe in the hot water

The health advice posted at Japanese onsen (based on a Ministry of the Environment notice) says ([Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://www.hokeniryo.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/kankyo/eisei/hotspring/riyou.html)):

- **Don't bathe right before or after a meal, or after drinking.** Never bathe drunk.
- **Drink about a glass of water** before and after bathing.
- **Keep it short at first:** about 3–10 minutes, building up to 15–20 minutes once you're used to it.
- **If you're older, or have high blood pressure, heart disease or a past stroke,** avoid baths of 42°C or hotter ([Japan Onsen Association](https://www.spa.or.jp/onsen/1632/)).

## Travelling with kids?

Young children can go into the parent's side, but there is an age limit set by each prefecture. In **Tokyo**, children aged 7 and over may not enter the opposite-sex bath (lowered from 10 on January 1, 2022) ([Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://www.hokeniryo.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/kankyo/eisei/yokujyou/yokujyou_faq)). **Shizuoka** (which includes Atami and Izu) made the same change on October 1, 2023 ([Shizuoka Prefecture](https://www.pref.shizuoka.jp/kenkofukushi/eiseiyakuji/eiseionsen/1040424/1053060.html)). Other areas may differ, so ask the front desk.

## Have a tattoo?

Some baths refuse guests with tattoos, some let you in with cover stickers, and some welcome everyone. See our [tattoo-friendly onsen guide](../tattoo-friendly-onsen-japan/) for the rules at each place.

## Still can't face a shared bath?

That's fine. A **private bath (kashikiri)** is a bath you book for yourself or your group, so there are no strangers at all. Many ryokan have them.
