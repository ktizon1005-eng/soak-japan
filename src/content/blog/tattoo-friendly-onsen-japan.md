**Quick answer:** you can bathe in Japan with tattoos, but the rules depend on the place. Some baths let you in as you are, some let you in only if you cover the tattoo with their own stickers, and some still refuse anyone with ink. Below are the rules for well-known places, taken from each facility's official website on **October 9, 2026**.

Rules change. Before you go, open the official link for the place you pick and check again.

## Why some baths say no

Many Japanese baths have long refused people with tattoos because of the old link between tattoos and organised crime. In 2016 Japan's tourism agency asked bath operators to think about foreign visitors with tattoos and listed ways to let them in: cover stickers, no action for small tattoos, quieter bathing times, and private baths ([official notice, archived by Fukushima Prefecture](https://www.pref.fukushima.lg.jp/sec/21045e/irezumi.html)). That is why many places now have a "sticker rule" instead of a flat ban.

Japan's official tourism site gives the same advice we do: some onsen have a no-tattoo policy, so check with the facility first ([JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/see-and-do/onsen/)).

## The three kinds of rules

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Tattoo OK** | Walk in as you are. |
| **OK with cover stickers** | Buy the facility's stickers at the front desk and cover the tattoo completely. If your tattoo is too big to cover, you can't go in. |
| **No tattoos** | Not even with stickers. Pick somewhere else. |

## Tattoo OK

### Kinosaki Onsen (Hyogo)

The best-known tattoo-friendly onsen town. The official Kinosaki tourism site says all seven public baths in town allow tattoos of all sizes. Baths inside ryokan may have different rules, so check with your ryokan ([Visit Kinosaki](https://visitkinosaki.com/plan/visitor-info/guides/tattoos-in-kinosaki-onsen/)).

### Daikoku-yu (Oshiage, Tokyo)

A neighbourhood sento near Tokyo Skytree. Its official site says "tattoo OK". Closed on Tuesdays ([Daikoku-yu](https://www.daikokuyu.com/services.html)).

## OK with cover stickers

### Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club (Tokyo)

Tattoos are normally refused, but you can enter if the tattoo fits under **up to two of the facility's cover seals (each up to 11 × 20 cm)**. You must tell the staff first and keep the seals on for your whole visit ([official tattoo page](https://tokyo-toyosu.manyo.co.jp/tatoo-info/)).

### Thermae-yu (Shinjuku, Tokyo)

The rule is different for men and women ([official guide](https://thermae-yu.jp/user-guide/)):

- **Men:** only foreign tourists may enter. You need to wear one of the facility's stickers and show your passport or air ticket. If you arrived in Japan more than 90 days ago, you will be refused.
- **Women:** fashion tattoos are allowed on a trial basis. Tattoos larger than 30 × 30 cm must be covered with stickers (¥330 each).

### Hakone Yunessun (Hakone)

Yunessun has two areas, and since October 1, 2025 the rules are ([official notice](https://www.yunessun.com/news/detail/?CN=413712)):

- **Swimsuit area:** OK if the tattoo is covered, for example with a rash guard.
- **Mori no Yu (the traditional, no-swimsuit baths):** OK only if two of the facility's paid cover seals cover the tattoo completely.

### Solaniwa Onsen (Osaka)

Tattoos are refused in principle, but a cover-sticker trial is running: if you cover the tattoo completely with stickers sold at the facility, you can enter. Since December 1, 2023 there is no limit on how many stickers you use ([official FAQ](https://solaniwa.com/faq/)).

## Call ahead: Arima Onsen Kin no Yu / Gin no Yu (Kobe)

Many blogs call these tattoo-friendly, but the official website says nothing about tattoos, so we can't confirm it. Call before you go: 078-904-0680 ([official site](https://arimaspa-kingin.jp/)).

## Tips before you go

- **Check on the day you plan.** Every rule above can change; the official links are there so you can confirm.
- **Buy the facility's own stickers.** Places with a sticker rule sell their own at the front desk; the rules above refer to those.
- **Know your tattoo's size.** Sticker rules depend on whether it can be covered completely. Measure it before you travel.
- **Be honest at the desk.** Toyosu Manyo Club, for example, asks you to tell staff before entering.
